Each of the 8 economic extrapolations from the valence framework, computed with realistic parameter values and visualized. These are illustrative calculations, not empirical tests — they show what the equations predict when you plug in numbers, and make the structural claims visible.

1. The Sunk Capacity Equation

Departure Cost = Σ_i (C_shed,i / D_i) · R_i
ParameterValueMeaning
C_shed10–50Capacity shed (units of investment)
D_i0.2–0.95Integration depth (how hard to rebuild)
R_i8–80Replacement cost per unit

What this shows: Deeply integrated capacities (D > 0.7) dominate the total departure cost — you can't just count what you've invested, you have to weight by how hard each piece is to rebuild. Career change and firm exit have the highest costs because they shed the most deeply-integrated capacities.

2. The Dual Trap Function

Lock-in(t) = S(t) × V(t)
ParameterValueMeaning
S(t)0–1Structural trap — capacity already reallocated (CDI)
V(t)0–1Motivational trap — reward calibration to niche

What this shows: The multiplicative interaction means lock-in is not the sum of structural and motivational traps. Either one alone is escapable. Deep lock-in requires both to be high — the product creates a sharp threshold, not a gradual slope.

3. The Specialization Risk Premium

r_specialist = r_generalist + λ·CDI − μ·F_c

What this shows: The contour plot reveals the interaction valence adds to CAPM. High CDI increases required return (specialization risk), but conservation forces (cash reserves, diversification) can offset it. The break-even line shows where the premium vanishes. CAPM only sees the horizontal axis — valence adds the vertical dimension.

4. Niche Saturation Dynamics

dS/dt = α·S·(1−S/K) − δ·S·E(t), where dK/dt = γ·S (cultural substrate)
ParameterValueMeaning
α0.3Growth rate
δ0.05Environmental pressure coefficient
K (ecological)100 (fixed)Carrying capacity — finite for ecological
γ0.01Capacity expansion rate — positive for cultural
E(t)0 → 2.0Environmental pressure (onset at t=20)

What this shows: The core of valence's Prediction 3. Ecological substrates (finite K) show negative diversity-dependence — they saturate and decline under pressure. Cultural substrates (expanding K) show positive diversity-dependence — they accelerate because growth creates new niches. Same equation, different substrate → opposite prediction.

5. Portfolio Entrenchment

Entrenchment = (illiquid / total) × integration depth
ParameterValueMeaning
illiquidity0.1–0.9Fraction of assets that are illiquid
integration depth0.2–0.95How many functions each position serves

What this shows: Two portfolios with identical illiquidity ratios can have very different entrenchment scores — the integration depth dimension is what standard portfolio theory misses. The isoliquidity curves (dashed) show that moving up the y-axis (more integrated positions) increases entrenchment without changing liquidity at all.

6. Human Capital Depreciation

H(t) = H₀·e^(−ρt) + Σ_j w_j(t)·S_j
ParameterValueMeaning
H₀100Initial generalist capital
ρ0.05Generalist decay rate (5%/year)
w_j2.0/yearSpecialist accumulation rate

What this shows: The crossover point where specialist capital exceeds depreciated generalist capital is the rational commitment threshold — before it, you should maintain generalist skills; after it, you should specialize. This is missing from Becker/Mincer models, which don't include generalist decay. The crossover also explains why mid-career switching fails: you've passed the threshold and can't rebuild H₀ fast enough.

7. Technology Adoption Lock-In

Switching Cost(t) = C₀ + ∫₀^t Σ_i r_i(τ)·D_i dτ
ParameterValueMeaning
C₀10Base switching cost (setup)
r_i1.5/yearInvestment rate per period
D_i0.2 / 0.5 / 0.9Integration depth (API / mixed / embedded)

What this shows: Switching cost grows monotonically — it never decreases. The rate of growth depends on integration depth: API-based architectures (low D) remain switchable indefinitely; embedded/proprietary architectures (high D) become intractable within ~15 years. This is why dominant technologies persist despite superior alternatives — the integral makes departure cost cumulative.

8. Conservation Force Decay

F_c(D) = F₀·e^(−β·D)
ParameterValueMeaning
F₀100Initial conservation force
β2.0Decay rate with commitment depth

What this shows: (a) Conservation forces decay exponentially with commitment depth — cash reserves, diversified revenue, and transferable skills erode as you specialize. The "too big to fail" zone shows where political F_c (bailouts, grandfathering) replaces structural F_c — the firm is fragile but kept alive. (b) Survival depends on the product F_c × CDI, not either alone. High CDI with low F_c = vulnerable; high CDI with high F_c = survives.
