# THE FAMILY-KINDS DISCUSSION — A GENEALOGY

This is the companion to the complementarity lineage (Bohr → Delbrück →
Elsasser/Pattee/Rosen → Stent). That lineage supplies the *epistemology*
(observation is naked, representation is theory-laden, programs need not
commute). This lineage supplies the *definitory apparatus* for working
in domains whose boundaries cannot be exactly defined — the
instantial-boundary-challenged condition.

The two are the same austerity at two levels: Bohr says representations
don't commute; the family-kinds discussion says *definitions* don't
collapse to a single essential trait. Both say: the boundary is real,
it just isn't what the classical apparatus expected it to be.

---

## 0. The problem statement

Some concepts resist monothetic definition: no single trait is present in
all members, yet membership is not arbitrary. Biology's canonical case is
**species**. The oldest family-kinds question — "can a class lack an
essence and still be a real class?" — has a named, traceable lineage.

---

## 1. Wittgenstein — family resemblance (1953)

**Ludwig Wittgenstein, *Philosophical Investigations* §§65–71** (1953). ★

- "Games": no common essence; the members are linked by overlapping,
  criss-crossing similarities — A shares features with B, B with C, but
  no feature runs through all.
- He called this **family resemblance** (*Familienähnlichkeit*).
- The consequence that matters here: a class can be *perfectly well
  defined* by its network of overlaps even when it has no essential
  trait. Definition-by-essence is one kind of definition, not the only
  kind.

---

## 2. Beckner — polytypic classes in biology (1959)

**Morton Beckner, *The Biological Way of Thought*** (1959). ○

- Imported the Wittgensteinian structure into biology as the
  **polytypic class** (later standardized as *polythetic*): a class whose
  members need not share any single defining trait, only a majority of a
  set of traits.
- The direct biological reading of §65–71, applied to taxa.

---

## 3. Sokal & Sneath — operational polythetic groups (1963)

**Robert R. Sokal & Peter H. A. Sneath, *Principles of Numerical
Taxonomy*** (1963). ○

- Made polythetic grouping *operational*: cluster analysis / numerical
  taxonomy as the algorithm that recovers polythetic classes from data.
- The computational realization of family resemblance — the ancestor of
  every "does this instance belong?" gate the foundry runs.

---

## 4. The modern species discussion (1970s–2000s)

The species-problem literature that Kull surveys (and that Ed's quoted
passage walks through). Each node is a distinct *definitory apparatus*
for a boundary-challenged concept:

- **Mayr (1942/1963) — Biological Species Concept**: species as
  actually/potentially interbreeding natural populations, reproductively
  isolated. Definition by *interaction* (gene flow), not shared traits.
  ○
- **Ghiselin (1974, "A Radical Solution to the Species Problem")**:
  species are **individuals, not classes** — historical particulars, not
  natural kinds. The escape from essentialism by changing the ontology
  (a species is more like an organism than like a set). ○
- **Paterson (1985) — Recognition Concept**: species as a shared
  **mate-recognition system** — the species is held together by a
  communication convention. The *semiotic* reading of species, and the
  direct ancestor of Kull. ○
- **Templeton (1989) — Cohesion Concept**: species as the most inclusive
  population with potential genetic/demographic cohesiveness. ○
- **de Queiroz (2007, "Species Concepts and Species Delimitation")**:
  the **unified species concept** — all species concepts agree that a
  species is a *metapopulation lineage*; the many "concepts" are not
  competing definitions but different **operational criteria** for
  delimiting that lineage. The reconciliation that ends the "incompatible
  definitions" jumble. ○

---

## 5. The semiotic turn — Pigliucci, Kunz, Kull

- **Pigliucci (2003, "Species as Family Resemblance Concepts")**:
  species concepts themselves form a category on the basis of family
  resemblance — "Wittgenstein has posthumously solved the species
  problem." ○
- **Kunz (2012, *Do Species Exist?*)** — the observation Ed quoted:
  biological species possess no single trait present in all members;
  species cannot be defined by essential traits; therefore **cannot be
  natural kinds**; a polythetic class, not a monothetic one. ○
- **Kull (2016, "The Biosemiotic Concept of the Species")** — the
  passage Ed quoted in full. Kull's move: the boundary is still *real*
  even though it is not essential — it is **semiotic**, fixed by
  recognition/communication compatibility. And his key consequence: "if
  we are going to apply a semiotic approach, the non-Aristotelian
  categories become unavoidable." ★ (passage in hand)

**What Kull adds that the earlier nodes don't:** the reason
non-Aristotelian categories are *unavoidable* rather than merely
convenient. If living units are semiotic agents (see Kull & Favareau),
then every boundary a living unit casts — from the membrane outward —
inherits the semiotic character: defined from the inside, by the unit's
own recognition/communication, not by an external essential trait. The
membrane (exact boundary, Jordan-curve-sharp) and the species
(polythetic boundary, semiotically held) are the two ends of one scale:
**the co-original barrier**. Inside/outside is decided by the boundary
itself at every level; the boundary just becomes *instantial* rather
than *essential* as the aggregate grows.

## 6. Where the foundry sits

The foundry's own cross-domain concepts — **the seeking dynamic, the
conserved core, ordered loss, substrate independence** — are themselves
instantial-boundary-challenged. They are family-resemblance categories:
present in many instances, not essential in all.

The methodological consequence (this is the operational posture):

1. **Operational predicates within each domain.** Define the concept
   operationally per domain — a fitted biphasic model (T3), a PGLS slope
   (T1), a permutation ordering (T6), a cross-kingdom transfer (L3).
   These are monothetic *within* their apparatus.
2. **Let the cross-domain pattern show itself.** Do not force one
   essential definition across domains. The pattern that recurs across
   non-commuting programs *is* the family resemblance — Bohr's naked
   observation at the aggregate scale.
3. **A non-instantiating case is a boundary probe, not a refutation.**
   When a domain fails to instantiate the pattern, the question is
   "what does this boundary tell us about the concept's domain of
   validity?" — not "the concept is false." This is exactly how the
   fit_biexp degenerate cases are handled (see
   `foundry-phased-breakdown.md`): an unresolvable fast phase at
   a given sampling scale is a measurement of the boundary, not a
   contradiction of the biphasic pattern.
4. **The claim-evidence conformance standard.** Because the concepts are
   boundary-challenged, the *label* must be scrupulously honest about
   what the *contents* establish: every claim reproducible (CI gates,
   baseline oracle), every citation pointing at a real passage, and the
   representation openly theory-laden while the observation is left
   naked for anyone to re-represent in their own vocabulary.

## 7. The full chain (one line)

Wittgenstein (family resemblance, 1953) → Beckner (polytypic classes,
1959) → Sokal & Sneath (operational polythetic groups, 1963) → Mayr
(interaction-defined species, 1942/63) → Ghiselin (species as
individuals, 1974) → Paterson (recognition concept, 1985) → Templeton
(cohesion, 1989) → de Queiroz (unified concept = lineage, 2007) →
Pigliucci (species concepts as family resemblance, 2003) → Kunz (no
essential trait, polythetic, 2012) → Kull (semiotic species, 2016) →
**the foundry's boundary-probe methodology.**

And the parallel chain (see the complementarity lineage):
Bohr (complementarity, 1932) → Delbrück → Elsasser / Pattee / Rosen /
Stent → **the foundry's empirical posture.**

The two chains meet at one point: **the boundary is real, it is
semiotic/instantial rather than essential, and it is probed, not
assumed.**
