1. The Sunk Capacity Equation

Departure Cost = Σi (Cshed,i / Di) · Ri

Claims: The cost of leaving a commitment is the sum, across all shed capacities, of (capacity shed ÷ integration depth) × replacement cost. Deeply integrated capacities cost more to rebuild; lightly integrated ones are cheap.

Replaces: Sunk cost fallacy (Arkes & Ayton, 1999), escalation of commitment (Brockner, 1992), endowment effect (Kahneman et al., 1991). Reframes persistence as rational assessment of structural departure cost, not cognitive bias.

2. The Dual Trap Function

Lock-in(t) = S(t) × V(t)

Claims: Niche lock-in has two independent components: structural trap S(t) = CDI(t) (capacity already reallocated) and motivational trap V(t) (reward-detection system calibrated to niche peak). Both must be high for deep lock-in. Either alone is escapable.

Replaces: Hedonic treadmill (Brickman & Campbell, 1971), status quo bias (Samuelson & Zeckhauser, 1988), learned helplessness, Goodness Paradox (Wrangham, 2019).

3. The Specialization Risk Premium

r_specialist = r_generalist + λ · CDI − μ · F_c

Claims: Required return for a specialized strategy equals generalist return plus irreversibility premium (λ·CDI) minus conservation-force credit (μ·F_c). As CDI → 1, premium → λ. As F_c → 0, premium increases even at stable CDI.

4. Niche Saturation Dynamics

dS/dt = α · S · (1 − S/K) − δ · S · E(t), where K is endogenous to substrate type (finite for ecological, expanding for cultural: dK/dt = γ·S)

Claims: The sign of dK/dt determines the sign of diversity-dependence. Ecological substrate (finite K) → negative DD. Cultural substrate (expanding K) → positive DD. This is the mathematical core of valence's Prediction 3.

Replaces: Logistic growth (Verhulst, 1838), endogenous growth (Romer, 1990), network effects (Metcalfe's Law), market saturation theory.

Cross-Cutting Findings

Strengths

  • All 8 extrapolations derive from the same valence equations — substrate-independence is the core claim, and the math transfers cleanly.
  • The falsification criteria are bidirectional — falsifying economic predictions weakens biological substrate-independence.
  • The reframings are non-trivial: sunk cost as rational, welfare states as F_c management, Solow→Romer as substrate shift.

Weaknesses

  • No original economic data in any of the 8. All rely on reinterpreting existing literature. WCI empirical support scores are uniformly low (20–55).
  • CDI operationalization is the bottleneck. Every extrapolation requires measuring Commitment Depth Index in economic systems. No operationalization protocol exists. This is the single highest-priority need.
  • V(t) (motivational trap) is not operationalized in economic terms. The dual-trap function (§2) and the brand loyalty / Stockholm syndrome predictions depend on it.
  • Integration depth for financial assets (§5) needs specification — what is Di for a portfolio position?

Priority Actions Before Simulation

  1. Operationalize CDI for at least one economic domain (firms or careers)
  2. Identify datasets: NLSY/PSID for §6, industry entry/exit for §4, IT migration for §7
  3. Resolve V(t) operationalization or defer §2 and §5 to a later round
  4. Run simulations for Tier 2 extrapolations (§1, §3, §4, §6, §7, §8) — these are the ones where the math is sound and the predictions are falsifiable
